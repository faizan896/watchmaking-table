01 — The idea

I loved watches long before I could make anything.

I knew the brands, the founders, the famous calibres. I could name them before I understood a single word of what I was actually looking at.

Then I started watching watchmakers work. Hours of it. Someone's hands under a lamp, setting a bridge, turning a screw a quarter turn and stopping. I could explain what an escapement does. I had still never made anything.

That's the gap this is for. I had the eye but no hands, no CAD, no bench. So I built the bench.

02 — The calibre

Everything you ask about
a watch is decided here.

How thick is it? That depends on the winding. A full rotor adds about a millimetre. Hand-wound and it can be properly thin. How does the seconds hand move? That's the beat rate: five steps a second, or ten. If it glides smoothly, it isn't mechanical at all.

So the movement is Chapter 02, not an afterthought. The nine chapters after it are all downstream of what you choose here.

Architecture
Separate bridges, three-quarter plate, full plate, skeletonised
Winding
Full rotor, micro-rotor, peripheral, or hand-wound
Frequency
18,000 · 21,600 · 28,800 · 36,000 vph
Finishing
Côtes de Genève, perlage, frosted, straight graining
Worked out for you
Thickness, lug-to-lug, water resistance

03 — The process

  1. Pick the movement.Architecture, winding, beat rate, finishing. That settles how thick the watch can be.
  2. Build the case round it.Shape, size, crystal. Keep an eye on lug-to-lug: that's the number that decides whether it fits you.
  3. Put a dial on it.Fifty-six to choose from. Enamel, stone, marquetry, painted, or just a sentence.
  4. Choose the hands.First thing anyone notices. Worth leaving until you can see everything else.
  5. Take it with you.A spec sheet, a big image, or a link that rebuilds the exact watch for whoever opens it.

04 — The atelier

Somewhere to try movements
nobody asked for.

Nothing here is for sale. It's a bench. Every real decision a watchmaker makes, laid out so you can actually make something. If you pick a combination that wouldn't work in metal, it'll tell you why. You can ignore it.
